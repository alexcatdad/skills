# Botanical and culinary modeling techniques

## Flowering cherry

- **Crown and scale.** Inspect branching from the side and back. Rotating a main limb can leave a whole bundle of fine shoots parallel. Vary child takeoff angle, curvature and taper. Place flowering spurs along suitable older wood, measure blossom size against twig diameter, and vary cluster density independently of flower scale.
- **Blossoms.** Arrange floret centers in an umbel, then route the pedicels back to a short shared spur. Model the reddish calyx, overlapping notched petals and short curved stamens with staggered origins. A cluster becomes visibly mechanical when all filaments emerge from one point or end in identical gold beads. Photographed petal outlines and pigmentation make useful inputs; check the resulting surface edge-on for folds and spikes. [Inflorescence modeling](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/inflorescence.sig16.html) · [Yoshino reference collection](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/prunus-x-yedoensis/)
- **Bark.** For dark rings around a fork, compare clay, vertex-color, normal-only and albedo-only renders. If the rings appear in the color pass, visualize UV regions to see whether the limb samples the intended bark tile. Correct its mapping before increasing texture resolution. Check projected chart overlaps, filtering gutters and interpolation at wrap seams. Preserve crisp, isolated lenticels without repeating broad pigment patches down the trunk. Compare with an actual specimen, including its natural lichen. [Red Wing Park bark photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yoshino_cherry_tree_bark_1.jpg)
- **Roots and ground.** Show the collar at grade and the standalone mesh with the intended soil level identified. A swept trunk can leave a hemispherical endpoint below ground. Shape the transition into asymmetric structural roots. At grazing angles check that grass overlaps the flare and litter stays on visible terrain. Match scan tiling to its measured physical width. A panorama can supply the distant field while near-field geometry supports parallax and contact.
- **Focus and lighting.** If screen-space blur leaves sharp holes between branches, sample offset camera positions aimed at a shared focal plane and inspect the gaps again. Keep motion responsive with a pinhole preview and accumulate only at rest. Use the measured sky for lighting when available, and check crown-interior shading against exterior bunches.

## Shoyu ramen

- **Containment.** Derive the permitted noodle radius from the bowl's *inner* profile at each strand height. Allow for tube thickness. Clip-testing just the centerline against the rim misses strands that pierce the lower sidewall. Check generated vertices and sample triangle interiors. Inspect the front, sides and underside in the render.
- **Toppings are obstacles too.** A strand can clear the bowl wall and still intersect an egg. Route paths smoothly beneath the underside of each topping over its projected footprint. Recheck the cut face visually. Sampling clearances supports a diagnosis; it cannot prove arbitrary meshes are intersection-free.
- **Food materials.** Compare a real cross section under side light. For chashu, separate muscle fibers, irregular fat bands, braised exterior and slice thickness. For ajitama, align white and yolk surfaces to avoid a gray moat, vary the marinade gradient, and inspect whether the center reads as moist. For nori, use a thin profile and localized crinkling where the sheet enters broth. Broad regular noise can look like fabric.
- **Broth and noodles.** Refract the view of submerged strands, attenuate their contrast with depth, and provide plausible reflections from the same light illuminating the bowl. Judge an oil film by its glancing highlight and edge. A broad white patch can look cloudy. Build folded, crossing paths with slack and partial submersion; shifting a set of parallel curves leaves the bundle pattern visible.
- **Grounding and final inspection.** Seat the foot on the table and compare the narrow contact shadow to the broader cast shadow. After correcting intersections, inspect noodle wetness at the waterline, broth reflections and the cut surfaces of pork and yolk under both lights.
